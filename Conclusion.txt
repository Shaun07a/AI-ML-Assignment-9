This project demonstrates the use of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for classifying images of cats 
and dogs. The dataset was preprocessed by resizing images to 128 × 128 pixels, normalizing pixel values, 
and splitting the data into training and testing sets using TensorFlow data generators. The CNN 
architecture consisting of convolutional layers, pooling layers, a dense layer, and a sigmoid output layer 
successfully learned important visual features from the images. The evaluation metrics and confusion 
matrix indicate that the model achieved good classification performance. Convolution layers 
automatically extract meaningful image features, while pooling layers reduce spatial dimensions and 
computational complexity. Compared to traditional Artificial Neural Networks, CNNs are more effective 
for image classification because they preserve spatial information and automatically learn visual patterns. 
A limitation of CNNs is that they require large datasets and considerable computational resources for training.
